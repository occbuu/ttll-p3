# Gói biên tập Paper3

## Highlights

- Source-linked survey records are paired with historical bus and walking-network data.
- Stated walking discomfort is kept distinct from a single motorcycle-habit response.
- Spatial-block intervals show uncertainty around distance contrasts between groups.
- Later map coverage is assessed without treating map change as metro impact.
- Policy diagnosis must match access indicators to the barriers they can measure.

## Dự thảo cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider “Beyond distance to a bus stop: Stated barriers and historically mapped transit access in Ho Chi Minh City” for publication in the Journal of Transport Geography.

The manuscript examines a practical problem in transport planning: whether a short mapped distance to public transport is sufficient to dismiss a reported first-mile barrier. We link source-verified resident responses collected in 2023 to historical OpenStreetMap bus locations and a walking network reconstructed with explicit access and connectivity rules. The analysis retains the questionnaire's original barrier categories, documents source and map uncertainty, and reports spatial-block intervals and sensitivity to alternative definitions.

The contribution is a transparent case-based diagnostic procedure connecting survey interpretation, historical spatial data and policy evidence needs. The study does not claim a causal effect of metro operation or that infrastructure is unimportant. It identifies the additional service and route information required before a reported barrier can be translated into a policy intervention.

This is a secondary analysis of the Truong Tho Living Lab survey programme. Related manuscripts currently under review address stakeholder priority differences, spatial clustering of environmental appraisals, and flooding appraisals linked to environmental data. They share elements of the survey programme but have different research questions and primary analyses. Their details and copies will be supplied in accordance with the journal's related-work disclosure requirements. The present manuscript is intended for submission to one journal at a time.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.

Corresponding author information to be supplied by the authors before submission.
